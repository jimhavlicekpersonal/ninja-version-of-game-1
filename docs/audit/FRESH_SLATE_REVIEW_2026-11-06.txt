=====================================================================================
0. SCOPE, METHOD, LIMITS
=====================================================================================

Question asked: are memory use, loading, the GUI, the game theory, the psychological
hooks, general playability and profitability up to par; is the balance between
ninjas, buildings, army compositions and play styles sound; does the free (no-Jade)
curve feel "slightly tedious but not impossible"; is there a distinct advantage to a
smart loadout and to timing/grouping on the battlefield; are there several ideal
strategies for placing ninjas and building the base; will new ninja classes and
buildings break the game; what is left to polish for a worldwide release.

Method (fresh slate, prior conclusions in docs/ used only as pointers):
  - Read the sim (src/gameplay/sim/**), the data (src/gameplay/data/*.json), the UI
    and render code, the balance tools (tools/balance/*, tools/progression/*).
  - Ran the full test suite: 131 files passed, 1 skipped; 1139 tests passed, 14
    skipped (npm test, 64 s).
  - Ran the production build and headless Chromium measurements for boot, network
    profiles, texture totals and a CPU profile (tools/perf/lowend.mjs, profile.mjs).
  - Ran two battle experiments of my own against the real sim (scratch test,
    section 3) to measure (a) whether deployment timing and grouping change the
    outcome and (b) whether a base can hold at all.
  - Built Keep-by-Keep economy tables from the JSON data (section 3).
  - Three parallel sub-reviews (performance/GUI, hooks/monetization, economy/
    buildings) fed sections 2.6, 2.7 and 2.4; I verified their key claims against
    the code before including them.

Limits: everything below is emulation, data analysis and model-player simulation.
No real player data, no physical cheap phone, no signed store build, no revenue was
available. The campaign model player (tools/balance/playerModel.ts) uses the bots'
simple attack plan, never buys a builder, never spends Jade and never places walls,
so its numbers are a floor for a human, not a prediction.

Short verdict, per area (details in section 2):
  Memory/loading ........ Not yet up to par for 2 GB phones: one 35 MB island
                          texture (16 MB on "low"), 513 KB first download, 10-26 s
                          to first frame on 3G/2G, 49 useless locale chunks.
  GUI ................... Solid structure, good bottom bar, honest labels; a dozen
                          sub-44 px targets and 10-11 px fixed type; untested for
                          long translations.
  Game theory ........... A well-built, test-pinned power model, but the live
                          balance has three structural problems: air, support and
                          siege armies are dead strategies; defence (buildings and
                          walls) is close to irrelevant below a maxed base; timing
                          and grouping move the result by only a few points.
  Progression (free) .... Too fast in Keep levels (Keep 15 in ~4 months for a
                          4-session player), builder-bound and hollow from Keep 8,
                          and casual players lose more to bot raids than they loot.
                          "Slightly tedious but possible" is not yet where it sits.
  Hooks ................. Complete, honest, no dark patterns found; one clock
                          problem (UTC chest vs local calendar) and a tutorial
                          that nudges the Jade skip.
  Profitability ......... Ethical frame is right, but the Jade ladder is
                          internally inconsistent (three cheap products dominate
                          every pack) and the cosmetic catalogue is thin.
  Playability ........... Playable end to end; the first real choice arrives
                          only at Keep 3-4, the second-day Keep 3 dip remains.
  Worldwide ............. Blocked: 0 of 758 strings translated in all 49
                          non-English locales while the status file says
                          "50 languages"; several compliance texts bypass t().
  Future-proofing ....... Warriors: good (one formula, tests pin it). Buildings
                          and per-Keep economy tables: hand-written, five
                          parallel tables, no cost-efficiency budget for defences.

=====================================================================================
1. HOW THE GAME IS BUILT (facts the findings rest on)
=====================================================================================

Resources: Coins (acorns) pay for defences, walls, Keep and Farms; Provisions (dew)
pay for every warrior, every research, every economy and military building. Jade
(moonstones) is premium; Shadow Ink (nightsap) exists in data only.
Warriors: 50 live = 10 roles x 5 tiers (progression.json roles, roster.json).
Power per housing = 21 x role efficiency x 1.4^(tier-1) x 1.16^(level-1), with
HP:DPS split by role (hpPerDps). Tier n unlocks at Keep 1/4/7/10/13 and its level cap
is the Keep's cap minus 2 per tier (levelCapOffset), so at any Keep each tier at its
cap is ~4 % stronger per housing than the tier below, and ~18 % dearer to train.
Buildings: 25 live of 54 catalogued. Six shooting defences: Kunai Tower (ground,
range 7), Firework Mortar (ground splash, min range 3), Shuriken Tower (both, fast),
Sky Bow (air), Kite-Cutter Ballista (air, grounds flyers, Keep 8), Longbow Yagura
(ground, range 13, counter-artillery, Keep 12). Two traps, three "soft" counters
(Lantern Line, Repair Crane, Incense Brazier), walls capped at 125 from Keep 6 on.
Per-Keep counts barely grow: Keep 6 = 4 Kunai/3 Shuriken/2 Mortar/2 Sky Bow;
Keep 15 = 4/3/2/3 + 2 Ballista + 2 Yagura = 16 shooting defences for a 360-housing
army. One Dojo, three camps, up to five builders (two free, three for 250/500/1000
Jade; the player starts with 500 Jade).
Battle: 180 s, 10 ticks/s, deterministic. Stars: 1 for >= 50 % destruction, 1 for
the Keep, 1 for 100 %. Targeting is "nearest building of the preferred kind"; walls
are a path cost (hedgePathCost 10), not a hard block. Attackers deploy only inside
6 Infiltration Points (8 compass approaches minus 2 chosen by a hash of the layout),
each hiding ~1/3 of the army, so every attack is at least three squads. Smoke Bomb
(1 + housing/40 charges, max 3) and Sabotage are the only timed abilities.
Bot raids on the player: a slot every hour with 12 % chance (~2.9/day), a 12-16 h
shield after any raid >= 30 %, full army of the player's Keep at that Keep's
research cap, bot deployment plan; losses are 20 % of storage capped by
storageLootCapByHall plus 50 % of uncollected.

2. FINDINGS
=====================================================================================
Severity: CRITICAL = blocks a worldwide commercial release or breaks the core loop;
HIGH = materially hurts retention, revenue or fairness; MEDIUM = clear defect or
design gap; LOW = polish. "Overlaps backlog" marks items the 2026-11-05 audits already
list (docs/audit/STATE_OF_THE_GAME.md, docs/store/WORLDWIDE_AUDIT.md); the evidence
here is fresh.

------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 Warrior balance
------------------------------------------------------------------

B1  HIGH  Air, support-heavy and siege armies are dead strategies in the lab.
    Evidence: docs/game-theory/ARMY_BALANCE.md (generated from tools/balance/armyLab.ts,
    equal-housing armies at the Keep's caps vs mid-level generated bases, bot plan):
    Air never exceeds 56 % destruction and never three-stars at Keep 6+; support-heavy
    15-55 %, 0 % three-star at Keep 7+; siege 26-44 % at Keep 7+; melee rush falls to
    33-41 % at Keeps 8/12/15. Only "Mixed", "Melee+ranged light support" and
    "Artillery-heavy" are competitive, and at Keep 15 "Mixed" (84 %, 44 % three-star)
    dominates everything (next best 71 %).
    Cause (data): the Sky role pays twice. Its efficiency is 0.568 (progression.json
    roles.sky) so a flyer has half a Blade's power per housing, AND its training price
    per housing is ~5x a Blade's (troops.json: owl_bomber 400 dew / 4 housing = 100
    vs hedgehog_roller 20; at Keep 13 cap 5,119 vs 1,024 dew per housing). Three
    defences hit air (Shuriken, Sky Bow, Ballista) and Sky Bow is the most cost-
    efficient defence in the game (HP x DPS per Coin, section 3.4). Support (Mystic
    0.82) and Siege (0.9) pay an efficiency tax on top of needing a front line that
    the equal-housing compositions do not give them.
    Why it matters: "multiple ideal strategies" is the stated goal; today the data
    says one family of compositions (blades + throwers + a guardian line + a few
    saboteurs, i.e. the raid bots' own mix) is the answer at every Keep. The
    "Suggested army" button (sim/suggest.ts) hands that answer to the player, which
    is honest but confirms the mono-strategy.

B2  MEDIUM  Several newly unlocked tiers are weaker in raw stats than the tier below
    at the same Keep, which players will read as a downgrade.
    Evidence (section 3.1, computed at each Keep's research cap): thousand_needle
    (thrower t5) 110.9 power/housing vs chain_sickle_reaper (t4) 137.6 at Keep 13;
    siege_bombardier (siege t4) 139.5 vs bomb_carrier (t3) 189.6; hawk_tamer (beast
    t2) 149.0 vs hound_handler (t1) 184.8. These carry deliberate DPS taxes for range
    or utility (roster.json dpsScale), and the "no dominance" test only checks the
    budget, not the result. The lab (B1) shows the taxed families are the weak ones.
    Why it matters: a tier-5 warrior that loses to a tier-4 one in a plain fight
    needs its ability to be visibly worth it, and nothing in the UI explains the
    trade.

B3  MEDIUM  Tiers are vertical sidegrades with a steep cost premium, so the
    cheapest power per Provision is always "max the tier-1/2 core" and new tiers are
    a luxury for a free player.
    Evidence: at the pinnacle each tier is ~4 % stronger per housing than the one
    below (PROGRESSION_MODEL.md, verified in section 3.1) while training costs 2x per
    tier (progression.json training.tierCostMult) and research 3x per tier
    (research.tierCostMult). A tier-5 blade at its Keep-15 cap costs 5,735 dew/housing
    to train vs 2,924 for the tier-1 blade, for +18 % power.
    Why it matters: it is a defensible "legends are 1.2x initiates" design and it
    keeps old warriors alive, but it means research order is near-dominant and the
    excitement of unlocking a new tier is mostly cosmetic.

B4  MEDIUM  Mastery (warrior and building EXP bonuses up to +30 % / +15 %) is
    documented as part of the model but is not implemented.
    Evidence: PROGRESSION_MODEL.md "Mastery EXP" rows and progression.json mastery /
    buildingMastery constants; zero references to mastery in sim/village.ts or
    sim/battle/battle.ts; tests pin only the EXP formula.
    Why it matters: the doc promises a long-tail hook that does not exist, and the
    roster test "EXP needed grows without end" gives false confidence.

B5  LOW  The hero (Shadow Captain) is a free auto-join from Keep 7 whose level
    follows the Keep, with one aura. There is no hero choice, no hero levelling and
    the other three heroes are planned only (battle.json heroes, roster.json heroes).
    Why it matters: heroes are the genre's main "smart loadout" lever; one automatic
    hero adds power (+2 to +6 destruction measured by the previous pass) but no
    decision.

------------------------------------------------------------------
2.2 Building and defence balance
------------------------------------------------------------------

D1  HIGH  Defence is nearly worthless unless the base is maxed, and the free player's
    base is never maxed. (Overlaps backlog E26, with a new explanation.)
    Evidence: campaign.json / CAMPAIGN.md: 0 % of bot raids held at every Keep for all
    four policies over ~270 raids each, including the "defender" policy that builds
    every counter and prioritises defences; stolen per raid is identical with and
    without that policy (Keep 12: 2,696,331 vs 2,696,362).
    My experiment (section 3.2, same bot plan, cap-level mixed army of the Keep):
    generated bases at levelBias 0.5 (buildings ~1 level below cap, the state a
    real player is in most of the time) hold 0-33 % and are three-starred 0-67 %;
    the same layouts at levelBias 1.0 (maxed) hold 17-100 % and are never
    three-starred. So defences CAN hold; the problem is that attackers reach the
    Keep's research cap (one lane, 30 min to 2.5 days per level) far sooner than
    defenders reach building caps (7-day timers, 1,771 builder-days to max Keep 15,
    section 3.3). The attacker/defender timeline is asymmetric by roughly 3-13x in
    builder-days vs research-days at Keeps 8-15.
    Why it matters: a third of a free player's Coins from Keep 7 on goes into
    buildings that measurably do nothing for them, and the held-defence reward
    (raids.json defenseWin 5 %) is dead in practice (0 and 600 Coins paid over whole
    campaigns).

D2  HIGH  Walls are close to irrelevant and sometimes harmful.
    Evidence (section 3.2): removing every wall from a generated base changed mean
    destruction by -16 to +13 points depending on layout and Keep, with no consistent
    sign (Walled Keep at Keep 8 bias 0.5: 49 % with walls, 65 % without; the same
    layout at Keep 5: 67 % with, 59 % without; Keep 15 bias 1: 49 % with, 41 %
    without). Walls are a path cost of 10 (battle.json hedgePathCost), not a block,
    the wall cap is frozen at 125 from Keep 6 to 15 (hall.json), and only "walls"/
    "breach" roles ever target them (battle.ts chooseTarget).
    Why it matters: the genre's primary base-design tool (funnels, compartments,
    time-under-fire) barely exists, so "multiple ideal base layouts" cannot emerge
    from walls. The five archetypes differ in shape but measure within +/-8 % of each
    other (trials.json layoutPower 0.94-1.08).

D3  HIGH  Defence roles do not form a counter matrix. Five of ten attacker roles
    have no cost-relevant live counter.
    Evidence: buildings.json combat.targets and fortifications.ts. Air is countered
    three times over; stealth by the Lantern Line only (3 s reveal, +10 % damage);
    sabotage by the Repair Crane (2-3 uses); support by the Brazier (30 % cut);
    throwers by the Yagura from Keep 12 only (1.5 M Coins each); guardians, siege,
    beasts, infiltrators and blades face only generic towers. The Mortar, the nominal
    anti-swarm, is 5-10x less HP x DPS per Coin than a Kunai Tower at every Keep
    (section 3.4), so the dominant Coin spend is "Kunai Towers, then Sky Bows".
    The 29 planned buildings include exactly the missing counters (Whirling Blade
    Keep 4, Listening Jars Keep 5, Living Bamboo Wall Keep 6, Sleep Dart Keep 10...)
    with counters/counteredBy lists already authored in progression.json.
    Why it matters: without a counter matrix there is no reason for a defender to
    read the meta and no reason for an attacker to scout for anything but loot.

D4  MEDIUM  Too few defence buildings per Keep for the army sizes.
    Evidence: hall.json limits: 9 shooting defences at Keep 4 for 120 housing, 11 at
    Keep 6 for 240, 16 at Keep 15 for 360; the count table is essentially flat from
    Keep 6 on while army housing grows 1.5x and the roster goes from 14 to 50
    warriors. Difficulty scaling comes from building levels alone.
    Why it matters: a base with 16 towers and 60+ other buildings is mostly loot
    filler; one squad per infiltration point walks the perimeter nearly unopposed,
    which is what section 3.2's 80-94 % destruction on Kunai Ring and Open Court
    shows.

D5  MEDIUM  Infiltration Points are chosen by a hash of the layout, not by the
    defender.
    Evidence: sim/battle/infiltration.ts planInfiltration: `drop = layoutHash(base) %
    8`, closing that approach and its opposite. Moving any building anywhere changes
    which two sides are closed, with no feedback.
    Why it matters: the one mechanic that could make base design strategic (which
    approaches are open, which squads must come where) is effectively random from
    the defender's point of view. The attacker sees the points at scouting; the
    defender cannot design for them.

D6  LOW  Generated opponents never upgrade a building during a raid and scouting
    never shows traps; the defence side has no information game beyond layout.

------------------------------------------------------------------
2.3 Strategy depth: loadout, timing, grouping, base-building
------------------------------------------------------------------

S1  HIGH  Timing and grouping move the result by only a few points; composition and
    which side you enter from decide the battle.
    Evidence (section 3.2, EXP1: the bots' mixed army of the Keep vs 6 layouts x 4
    seeds, five deployment plans):
      Keep 6:  bot 56.6 %, all-at-once 53.4, staggered+smoke 61.5, two waves 51.2,
               spread over all 6 points 65.0 (1-star 92 %, 3-star 0 %).
      Keep 10: bot 75.8, all-at-once 73.8, staggered+smoke 75.8, two waves 62.6,
               spread 70.2.
      Keep 15: bot 83.5, all-at-once 85.1, staggered+smoke 86.7, two waves 68.8,
               spread 76.5.
    "Staggered" (guardians and saboteurs first, blades 2.5 s later, throwers 4.5 s
    later, three smoke bombs on the nearest towers) beats "everything at once" by
    1.6 to 8 points; a second wave at 30 s loses 5-15 points because the 180 s clock
    runs out; spreading over six points is the best plan at Keep 6 and the worst
    at Keep 10. The same army, same bases: the spread between the best and worst
    sensible plan is 10-18 points, mostly explained by the time limit and the
    entry side, not by squad composition per point.
    Why it matters: the user's goal is "a distinct advantage to timing and grouping".
    Today a competent plan gains roughly half a star over a careless one. Smoke is
    worth a few points; Sabotage is one 6 s jam per Saboteur. There is no
    rage/freeze/heal-type spell, no hero ability trigger, no "wait for the mortar to
    retarget" moment, because targeting is nearest-building and defences have no
    retarget delay or fire arc.

S2  MEDIUM  The attacker's interaction surface is small: tap a glowing ring to drop
    one warrior per tap (a held finger streams), tap for each smoke bomb, choose the
    order from a strip. There is no per-squad grouping, no "deploy N" gesture, no
    pause, no ability button for warriors (kits fire automatically), no replay speed
    other than in replays.
    Evidence: src/presentation/render/BattleScene.ts 460-489, battleHud.ts.
    Why it matters: at 300-360 housing a full deployment is 100-200 taps or a long
    hold; grouping is a matter of where you hold your finger, not a decision.

S3  MEDIUM  "Smart loadout" has one dominant answer per Keep (B1) and the game tells
    you what it is (Suggested army). Loadout decisions that remain: how many
    saboteurs for the layout, whether to bring siege for a Walled Keep, scouting
    with "Next" rerolls (2-4 % of loot per reroll, the dominant raiding micro-skill).

S4  LOW  The base-building side has no "designed for a counter" feedback loop:
    defence logs keep only 10 raids, there is no heat map of where attackers entered
    or which building fell first, no "your base held X of the last Y" summary.

------------------------------------------------------------------
2.4 Progression curve and free-player economy
------------------------------------------------------------------

P1  HIGH  Keep levels come far too fast for a 15-Keep ladder; the Keep is never a
    goal. (New framing of backlog E23/E25.)
    Evidence: CAMPAIGN.md, regular model (4 sessions/day, 2 builders, no Jade,
    bots' attack plan): Keep 5 day 4.2, Keep 8 day 23.4, Keep 10 day 32.6, Keep 12
    day 51.2, Keep 15 day 117.6. Casual (1 visit/day): Keep 10 day 120, Keep 15 day
    246. The next Keep's Coin cost in hours of that Keep's max production never
    exceeds 55 h after Keep 6 and falls to 19 h by Keep 14 (section 3.3); nothing but
    cost, a builder and the hall cap gates the Keep (village.ts upgradeQuote).
    Genre reference (assumption, not measured): Clash of Clans TH10 ~6-12 months
    free, max TH multi-year. Kagemura's Keep 10 at 1-4 months is 3-8x faster.
    Why it matters: a hollow Keep 15 base (D1) is the natural result; matchmaking
    corrects by research strength, but the ladder stops meaning anything.

P2  HIGH  From Keep 8 on the base is builder-bound, not resource-bound: storages
    fill, raiding becomes pointless, and the only lever left is Jade for time.
    Evidence (section 3.3): builder-days of newly allowed upgrades per Keep: Keep 8
    78, Keep 10 223, Keep 12 256, Keep 14 395, Keep 15 312; cumulative 1,771
    builder-days (590 days with 3 builders, 354 with 5). Resource-days for the same
    upgrades at max income: 7-10 per Keep. Cause: progression.json buildings
    timeGrowth 2.2 with maxSeconds 604800: every collector L15+, storage L15+, camp
    L14+, Kunai L14+, Sky Bow L10, Ballista L7+, Yagura L5+ is a flat 7 days, while
    costGrowth 1.45 only matches collector rate growth (Coin Mint payback is a flat
    ~155 h from L12 to L24).
    Why it matters: this is "tedious AND nothing to do", the opposite of "slightly
    tedious but possible". It also makes the Artisan's Bundle (Jade for a builder)
    the only purchase that matters, which is a time-gate sale, not a value sale.

P3  HIGH  Casual players lose more to bot raids than they loot; the raid tax is the
    real early wall. (Overlaps E26.)
    Evidence: CAMPAIGN.md totals: casual 582 M stolen vs 358 M looted (163 %);
    casual explorer 314 %; regular 64-66 %. Casual Keep 5->6 takes 28 days (Keep
    4->5 took 9): ~31 raids x ~98 k stolen vs ~140 k/day produced. Raids come 12 %
    per hour slot (~2.9/day), only shields stop them (12-16 h after a 30 %+ raid),
    and battleEnd drops the shield on every attack (village.ts).
    Why it matters: the player who plays least is punished most, exactly where D30
    retention is measured.

P4  MEDIUM  Research is the late-game wall, and Provisions carry three loads.
    Evidence (section 3.3): incremental research to bring every unlocked warrior to
    its cap: Keep 11 45 M, Keep 13 223 M, Keep 14 581 M, Keep 15 1,526 M
    Provisions; the full roster at Keep 15 costs 1.53 B vs 495 M for every Keep-15
    building upgrade. Provisions also pay for training (3.98 M per 360-housing
    refill at Keep 15) and for every economy/military building. Three lanes from
    Keep 11; 81 lane-days at Keep 15 alone.
    Why it matters: Coins pile up (P2) while Provisions are permanently short; a
    smart free player stops upgrading economy buildings (they cost Provisions) to
    research, which deepens P2.

P5  MEDIUM  Collector upgrades stop production for the whole timer, and the quote
    does not say so.
    Evidence: village.ts upgrade sets collector.accrueFrom = endsAt; resources.ts
    produces nothing until then. A Coin Mint L15 (7-day timer) costs 530 k plus
    ~1.59 M of lost production; true payback ~620 h instead of the 155 h implied.
    Six collectors each 7 days per level = offline a third of the time for a player
    who upgrades them.

P6  MEDIUM  Keep 3 is still the second-day dip and Keep 1-2 offer no choice.
    Evidence: CAMPAIGN.md Keep 3 rows: regular 1.50 stars, 0 % three-star; casual
    1.00 stars, 60 % won, honor negative. Keep 1 has one camp, one tower, two troop
    types; the first real choice (second camp, Mortar, traps, Saboteur/Smoke) is
    Keep 3-4, around day 1-3.

P7  LOW  Loot cap (storageLootCapByHall = generated min from Keep 7) makes hoarding
    safe (a maxed Keep 15 defender loses <= 2 % of storage per raid) and makes real
    player bases strictly worse targets than generated ones for an attacker.

P8  LOW  Economy sharp edges (none exploitable): cancel refunds (upgrade, research,
    training) go through addCapped and are silently lost at full storage; the
    "20 % raidable" rule (economy.json raidableStoredFraction) is shown nowhere;
    paid scouting "Next" at 2-4 % of loot is the dominant raiding micro-strategy.

------------------------------------------------------------------
2.5 Future-proofing (new classes, new buildings)
------------------------------------------------------------------

X1  GOOD  Warriors are generated from one formula and pinned by tests.
    progression.ts warriorStats, tools/progression/pacing.mjs and rolecut.mjs,
    progression-model.test.ts (budget within 3 %, growth within 4 %, no dominance
    inside a tier, newly unlocked tier not stronger than the tier below). Adding a
    role = one roles entry + one roster row per tier + a kit; the tests then say
    what broke. This is the right skeleton.

X2  MEDIUM  The budget test checks inputs, not outcomes. A new warrior that passes
    "same budget per housing" can still be useless (B1: Sky passes and loses) or
    dominant (Shadow at 1.2 efficiency is the best power per Provision at every Keep,
    section 3.1). army-lab.test.ts has upper gates (no army too strong) and the
    campaign gates hold the model player's stars, but there is no lower gate per
    family ("every family must reach X % destruction at every Keep in the lab").

X3  HIGH  Buildings and per-Keep economy are hand-written tables with no budget.
    buildings.json has 54 x up to 24 level rows written by a generator that is not
    in the repo's sim path; hall.json is 15 hand-edited rows; storageLootCapByHall,
    generatedLootByHall, rewardScaleByHall, scoutFeeByHall, topUpScaleByHall and
    powerSlackByKeep are six parallel per-Keep arrays that must agree by hand.
    There is no "strength per Coin relative to the Kunai Tower" budget for defences
    (D3: the Mortar is 5-10x dominated and nothing flags it). Adding a defence type
    does not force a rebalance; adding a Keep level or changing income touches six
    tables and 54 level rows.

X4  MEDIUM  Counter-matrix data exists only for planned buildings
    (progression.json buildingCatalog counters/counteredBy, building-matchups.test
    .ts) and not for the live ones, so the live game cannot be checked for "every
    attacker role has a counter".

X5  LOW  Infiltration cover and smoke charges derive from housing, so a new
    high-housing class automatically changes squad sizes; the 180 s clock and
    nearest-target rule are global constants that any slow or long-range class will
    strain (siege_bombardier already does).

------------------------------------------------------------------
2.6 Memory, loading, GUI (measured in headless Chromium; see section 3.5)
------------------------------------------------------------------

M1  HIGH  The island ground is one huge texture: 4080 x 2241 px (36.6 MB RGBA) on
    "high", 2720 x 1494 (16.3 MB) on "low", and its CPU canvas is kept for the
    session for hit tests that are never run on it (assets.ts 264-303, quality.ts
    210/223, iso.ts). It is 35 of 63 MB of texture pixels on high and 15.5 of 26.7 MB
    on low, and texture upload (WebGLTextureWrapper._processTexture) is 3.3 of 6.4 s
    of sampled boot CPU. On a 2 GB Android this is the single biggest memory item.

M2  HIGH  First visit transfers 513 KB gzipped (index chunk 250 KB gz carrying all
    gameplay JSON including 221 KB troops.json, all code-art painters, the sandbox
    tools and most panels; Phaser subset 173 KB gz). Emulated time to ready: good
    4G 2.7 s; weak 3G 10.2 s; slow 3G 22.3 s; 2G 25.9 s. The boot guard's "taking a
    long time" card fires at 25 s. Only Friends and Gang War panels are lazy.

M3  MEDIUM  50 locale chunks (8 KB each, 49 byte-identical because untranslated)
    are built, precached (129 KB gz, ~50 requests at service-worker install) and
    the device language's chunk is fetched on the critical path only to discover it
    is below the 80 % coverage threshold and fall back to English (i18n.ts 76-98,
    main.ts 181).

M4  MEDIUM  Service-worker precache fetches 512 px icons, the maskable icon, the
    Apple touch icon and the legal pages with cache mode "reload" (sw.template.js
    413): ~470 KB after first frame, competing with the server hello on 2G.

M5  MEDIUM  Boot path stalls on slow links: the art manifest fetch (cache
    "no-cache", 2.5 s cap) for a manifest that is empty today; a 1.5 s font wait with
    no preload, so canvas labels stay in the fallback font on 3G; the language
    probe (M3).

M6  MEDIUM  Backdrop blur (4 px) behind every modal while the village keeps

2.7 Psychological hooks, monetization, profitability, honesty
------------------------------------------------------------------

H1  GOOD  Every return hook is present and honest. Verified in code: daily chest
    with odds shown first, seeded per day, overflow reported; streak with a free
    weekly skip, never wiped, never sold; 28-day calendar at local midnight; adaptive
    daily/weekly tasks; 30-day Season Scroll with a free track and a no-sale window
    in the last 4 days; monthly leagues; a 14-day event loop with real countdowns;
    endless Trials paid once per tier; weekly Alliance War; Gang Wars; settle-the-
    score rematch; Clan Stipend; notifications opt-in, per type, quiet hours,
    4/day. No fake urgency, no pay-to-remove-pain (Rebuild Kit retired), no offers
    to minors or unknown ages, prices only in the Store with a confirm, generated
    opponents labelled everywhere.

H2  HIGH  The Jade ladder is dominated by three cheap products, which caps ARPPU
    and makes the "+N % value" chip misleading in spirit.
    Evidence: store.json packs pay 45.5 -> 75.0 Jade per dollar ($0.99 -> $99.99);
    Clan Stipend 360 Jade for $2.99 = 120/$ and re-buyable; Starter Pack ~184
    Jade-equivalent per dollar; Artisan's Bundle 130/$; Keep Celebration ~143/$ and
    repeats every Keep. The pack cards compute "+N % value" against the $0.99 pack
    only (economyUi.ts 258-267). A value-rational adult never buys the four big
    packs.

H3  MEDIUM  Two clocks: the daily chest and stipend roll at UTC midnight
    (daily.ts 38-45, stipend.ts 33, refusal text says "(UTC)") while tasks and the
    calendar roll at local midnight (calendar.ts, pass.ts). In Jakarta the chest
    flips at 07:00, in Los Angeles mid-afternoon. Event end times print as UTC.

H4  MEDIUM  The tutorial highlights the gold "Finish now" (Jade skip) button twice
    (tutorial.ts finishKeep, finishTrain) before the age band is asked (hud.ts
    459-472), so minors are guided into a Jade spend on day one. The spend is tiny
    (2 Jade of a 500 start) but it trains the habit.

H5  MEDIUM  Competitive advantage of money is time only and bounded (nothing
    paywalled; minors capped at $50/month; Jade buys skips and top-ups), but the
    cheapest competitive lever is "another army now": finishing a full Keep-8 army
    costs ~67 Jade (~$1) and yields a raid worth ~1,100 Jade of top-ups (16x, where
    TOP_UP.md claims <= 10x).

H6  MEDIUM  Cosmetic depth is thin: six Keep looks, no warrior/hero skins, no
    village scenery, no battle effects, no clan banners, no cosmetic pass tier. In
    an ethically constrained economy (no random sales, no ads) cosmetics are the
    main non-competitive revenue line and it is almost empty.

H7  MEDIUM  Session structure: Keep 1 has no meaningful choice; the Keep 5->6
    save-up stretch (500 k Coins, ~10 raids) is a static "save X" objective for days
    with two builders; at Keep 10 the natural purchase is a builder (P2). The
    next-objective chip (objective.ts) never suggests anything paid, which is good.

H8  LOW  The minor cap is counted in catalog USD, not local store proceeds (already
    GLOBAL-007); the "Suggested army" is also the bots' army, so the player and the
    computer field the same thing.

4. SUGGESTIONS
=====================================================================================
All numbers are starting points to run through npm test, npm run balance:armies and
npm run balance:campaign; every one lives in data files. Combat changes bump
SIM_RULES and need npm run rules:snapshot first.

------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1 A smooth free curve: "slightly tedious but possible"
------------------------------------------------------------------
Target for a free player with three 10-minute sessions a day and the third builder
from starting Jade: Keep 5 ~7 days, Keep 8 ~45 days, Keep 10 ~90 days, Keep 12
~160 days, Keep 15 ~300-360 days, with a base that is roughly 80 % of its caps when
the next Keep opens.

  1. Make the Keep the goal again (P1). Multiply elder_toadstool cost by ~1.6 at
     Keep 8, 2.0 at Keeps 9-10, 2.5 at 11-12, 3.0 at 13-15 (Keep 10 5.45 M -> ~11 M,
     Keep 15 24 M -> ~72 M; storage still holds it at <= 0.8 of cap). Early game:
     double Keep 4-6 (20 k -> 40 k, 100 k -> 200 k, 500 k -> 800 k) so Keeps 3-5 are
     1-3 days each instead of hours, and the second-day dip (P6) lands on a base
     with a Mortar and traps.
  2. Add a soft gate in hall.json (levels[].requires, e.g. Dojo and two defence
     types at the current Keep's maxLevel) and surface it in the objective chip, so
     a hollow rush is a choice the game names, not the default.
  3. Turn the late game from builder-bound to resource-bound (P2, P5):
     buildings.maxSeconds 604800 -> 259200 (3 days) and timeGrowth 2.2 -> 2.0, so
     Keep 15's 312 builder-days become ~150; in exchange costGrowth 1.45 -> 1.55
     from level 12 so cost outgrows income (collector payback ~250-300 h at L24
     instead of a flat 155 h). Let collectors keep producing at the old rate during
     an upgrade (bank at completion) or print the lost production in the quote.
  4. A fourth builder without Jade: hall.json count stays 5, but allow a Coin price
     for the 4th hut at Keep 8 (economy.json builderHutCosts gains a {acorns} entry).
     Keep the 5th for Jade. This is the single largest free-player tedium lever.
  5. Stop the casual raid tax (P3): shieldTiers 12/14/16 h -> 16/20/24 h; a minimum
     gap between bot raids (raids.json minHoursBetweenRaids ~8); storageLootCapByHall
     at Keeps 5-8 cut to ~60 % of the generated minimum (60 k -> 20 k, 130 k -> 50 k,
     160 k -> 100 k, 220 k -> 130 k); raise collectorHoursCap 8 -> 12 so a one-
     session player banks half a day.
  6. Research (P4): research.tierCostMult 3.0 -> 2.2 (tier-5 total 72 M -> ~27 M;
     Keep-15 roster 1.53 B -> ~0.7 B); a 4th lane at Keep 13; price tier 3+ research
     in Coins (or alternate Coins/Provisions by tier) so both currencies carry two
     loads instead of one and three.
  7. Give defence a return once D1-D3 are fixed: raids.defenseWin.shareOfRaidLoot
     0.05 -> 0.25 and pay it on <= 1 star lost, not only 0; show "held X of your
     last Y raids" on the Keep sheet.
  8. Keep 1-2: add one choice (a second warrior at Keep 1, the first trap at Keep 2)
     so the first meaningful decision arrives in the first session.

------------------------------------------------------------------
4.2 A distinct advantage to a smart loadout, timing and grouping
------------------------------------------------------------------
The sim is deterministic and server-replayed, so every lever below is a data or
rule change that keeps replays exact.

  1. Make timing matter by giving defences behaviour that rewards waiting:
     a retarget delay (defences take 0.5-1 s to swing to a new target), fire arcs
     or a reload on the Mortar that a squad can rush between shots, and a Sky Bow
     that can only track one flyer for N ticks. Each creates a "go now" moment.
  2. Give the attacker 2-3 timed decisions per battle beyond Smoke: a Rally horn
     (the hero's aura on demand, not on first hit), a Caltrop-clearing charge, and a
     Decoy lantern that pulls targeting for 4 s. Charges from housing like Smoke;
     all deterministic.
  3. Grouping as a gesture: a "squad" picker (drag a slider for 5/10/20 of a type)
     and a per-point stagger (tap-and-hold shows a count). Keep one-per-tap for
     precision. This alone turns 150 taps into 10 decisions at Keep 15.
  4. Reward squads that match their point: each Infiltration Point already shows
     how many defences cover its path; add a per-point "entry bonus" the defender
     chooses (see 4.3), so the attacker's loadout per point is a real read.
  5. Fix the time budget: two-wave plans lose 5-15 points purely to the 180 s clock.
     Either 210 s at Keep 10+ (scales with housing) or a clock that only runs while
     warriors are deployed, so a planned second wave is viable.
  6. Rebalance the dead families (B1): raise Sky efficiency to ~0.68 and cut its
     training price per housing to ~2.5x a Blade's (today ~5x); give Mystics a
     stronger first-tier heal so a support line protects a Guardian line; give
     Siege wall damage a reason to exist by making walls matter (4.3). Add a lower
     gate per family to army-lab.test.ts ("every family >= 45 % destruction at every
     Keep, >= 10 % three-star at its own Keep") so the data cannot regress to one
     answer again.
  7. Keep the "Suggested army" but make it per layout (the scout report already
     knows the archetype): "for a Walled Keep: siege + guardians; for a Kunai Ring:
     saboteurs + throwers". Then the suggestion teaches reading, not copying.

------------------------------------------------------------------
4.3 Multiple ideal strategies for the base
------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. Make walls a real block (D2): raise hedgePathCost 10 -> 60 so only wall-
     breakers go through them, grow the wall count with the Keep (125 at Keep 6 ->
     200 at Keep 10 -> 275 at Keep 15), and add the planned Reinforced Gate (Keep 5)
     and Living Bamboo Wall (Keep 6) so a "compartment" base and a "funnel" base are
     both buildable.
  2. Let the defender choose which two approaches are closed (D5): replace the
     layout hash with a saved choice (a wall ring segment or a decorative gate
     building that marks "closed"), shown at scouting. Then Twin Gates vs Kunai Ring
     vs Open Court are the defender's decisions and the attacker's loadout per point
     answers them.
  3. Build the counter matrix (D3) from the catalogue already authored: Whirling
     Blade Tower (Keep 4, anti-swarm), Listening Jars (Keep 5, anti-tunnel/stealth),
     Living Bamboo Wall (Keep 6, anti-ram), Sand Bucket Post (Keep 7, anti-splash),
     Steam Vent (Keep 9, anti-stealth, clears smoke), Sleep Dart Tower (Keep 10,
     anti-big), plus a cheaper Yagura-like anti-thrower at Keep 8. Six buildings
     close every role. Give each live defence a counters: [...] list and a
     cost-efficiency budget (HP x DPS per Coin relative to the Kunai Tower, within a
     band per role) tested like progression-model.test.ts, and fix the Mortar to the
     band (today 5-10x under).
  4. Grow the defence count with the army (D4): roughly one shooting defence per
     20 housing (12 at Keep 4 -> 18 at Keep 15 in the count table), and raise the
     damage of the generic towers less steeply so counters, not numbers, do the
     work.
  5. Defence feedback (S4): keep 30 defence records, show the entry point and the
     first three buildings lost per raid, and a monthly "held %" on the Keep sheet.
  6. Three archetypes should each beat a different army: Walled Keep beats blades
     and beasts (siege needed), Kunai Ring beats guardians (saboteurs needed), Mortar
     Core beats swarms (shadow or air needed), Open Court beats slow armies (speed
     needed). Add this as a lab gate: each archetype must be at least 15 points
     better against its "wrong" army than against its "right" one.

------------------------------------------------------------------
4.4 Future-proofing new classes and buildings
------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. Keep the warrior formula and tests; add an outcome gate per family (4.2.6) and
     a "new class" checklist: efficiency, hpPerDps, housing, a kit, a counter
     building, a lab run, a campaign run.
  2. Generate buildings.json level rows from progression.json buildings (cost1,
     time1, power1 per type) the way pacing.mjs does for troops, and derive hall.json
     limits from buildingCatalog liveMaxLevel/pinnacleMaxLevel (buildingMaxLevelAt
     already exists). A new building then needs one catalogue row.
  3. Derive the six per-Keep tables (storageLootCapByHall, generatedLootByHall,
     rewardScaleByHall, scoutFeeByHall, topUpScaleByHall, powerSlackByKeep) from one
     "economy per Keep" number (max collector income per hour at that Keep) with a
     test that they agree. A new Keep level then is one row.
  4. Give every defence a role budget and counters list (4.3.3) with a test, and
     a lab table of defence-vs-family damage so a new building cannot silently
     dominate or be dominated.
  5. Scale global battle constants from data, not constants: battle duration,
     cover per point and smoke charges should read from the Keep (durationByKeep,
     cover share), so a slow or long-range class does not break the clock.
  6. Implement or remove mastery (B4) before building anything on it.

    a genre assumption. The consented statistics pipeline (step 5 of the backlog)

    from code and confirmed in emulation; the crash threshold on a 2 GB phone is
    not.

    timing levers to extract.
