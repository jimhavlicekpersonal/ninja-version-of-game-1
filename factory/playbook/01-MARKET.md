# Stage 1: gaps in the market

Everything after this stage serves the gap chosen here. Take the time.

## What this stage decides

Which player the next game is for, what that player cannot get today, where the game launches first, and how anyone will know whether the bet was right.

## What the chassis is, as a market fact

The template is an asynchronous base-building and raiding game: build a home base, gather two soft resources, train specialised units, scout and raid generated and real opponents, defend, join an alliance, climb leagues and an endless ladder. That genre is fixed. What is open:

- **Theme and fantasy:** who the player is, what world, what the units and buildings are.
- **Audience:** age, region, language, how and when they play, what they can pay.
- **Tone and promise:** cosy or fierce, comic or solemn, solitary or social.
- **Mechanical accents inside the extension points:** the signature ability, the specialist unit, the counters, the layout archetypes, the endless mode, events. The blueprint lists them.

Assets the template already has, which most competitors do not: a first visit under a megabyte and no image downloads; a low-end rendering profile for 1 to 3 GB phones; play that survives weak networks; 50 languages including several large ones the stores do not list; no paid randomness and honest labels; no forced login. Aim them at a real gap: "runs on a cheap phone in the player's own language, and does not cheat them" is a position in many markets where the genre leaders are 2 GB downloads in English.

## Method

1. **Read the research notes critically.** A chart position is a fact; a blog's revenue estimate is a guess. Keep the source and date with every claim.
2. **Generate theses widely before judging.** At least five. A thesis is audience × fantasy × region × reason-now. Include at least one that is not what you would have guessed before the research.
3. **Score each thesis** on: size of the unmet want; evidence for it; how crowded the theme is in this genre; fit with the template's assets; fit with the template's limits (it cannot become another genre); cost and risk of the content (cultural review, art difficulty, text difficulty across languages); how it would be found (store search terms, communities, creators); risk to the owner (IP proximity, regulation, payments in the region).
4. **Check the portfolio.** The new game must not repeat or compete with a game this line already made.
5. **Choose one.** Say why the runners-up lost. A thesis that only wins on taste did not win.
6. **Make it testable.** Launch waves from cheap-and-informative to expensive; the numbers that would confirm the choice (tutorial completion, first raid, day 1/7/30 retention, payer conversion, revenue per daily player, crash-free sessions) with targets, and the level below which spending stops and fixing starts.

## What went wrong last time

- The market was asked last. The audit's verdict: a serious build with no face, no story, no store assets, no soft-launch plan and no kill criteria.
- A ladder with no players shows only generated opponents on day one, honestly labelled; the first review says "it's all bots". The thesis must say how the cold start is survived (named rival factions fronting generated bases exist in the template; a region small enough to fill; friends features).
- Nobody decided what the free browser build is for. Decide here: a demo that hands off to the app, or the full game.
- Vendor benchmark figures (install costs, retention norms) were quoted as facts. Mark them as benchmarks and plan to replace them with the game's own numbers.

## Limits

- No thesis that needs paid randomness, advertising to children, or a market the compliance stage will have to exclude.
- No thesis whose appeal is being mistaken for an existing game.
- Statistics exist only for adults who opt in: every target number describes a biased sample; say so.

## A good record

A stranger can read the chosen thesis and name the player, the gap, the evidence and the first three things that would prove it wrong. The rejected theses are there with their scores. Unverified claims are listed as unverified.
