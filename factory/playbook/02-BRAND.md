# Stage 2: brand, name and appeal

## What this stage decides

The name, the world in a paragraph, the promises the game keeps, its voice, its cast, its icon idea and its store listing. Everything a player meets before the first tap, and the words every later stage must use.

## Method

1. **Start from the chosen player and gap.** The brand is the gap made visible: the store page should read as the answer to what that player could not find.
2. **Premise first, name second.** Three to five sentences: who the player is, what they are building, who stands against them, why it matters. If the premise is weak the name will not save it.
3. **Name candidates: at least six, keep the best three or more in the record.** A good name here:
   - fits the 30-character store title with a descriptive tail ("Name: What You Do");
   - can be said and typed by a player in every launch language, with no sound that language lacks;
   - means nothing rude, sad or silly in the launch languages, and is not a common surname, place or brand there;
   - is not the name, or one letter from the name, of a game, app, studio or famous work;
   - is searchable: not a common dictionary word alone;
   - still works as an app icon caption (about 12 characters).
4. **Use the conflict research** on the candidates, the cast names and the home-base word. Record what was found, even "nothing found in these places". The formal trademark search in the launch territories (software and entertainment classes) is an owner task, and the record keeps fallback names for the day it fails.
5. **World words.** Fix the words the whole game will use: the home base, the player's title, an alliance of players, generated rivals (their label must still say they are generated), the premium currency if the fiction needs one named here. Later stages copy these exactly.
6. **Cast with faces.** At least a mentor who teaches, a hero the player can field, and rival leaders who front the generated opponents. Each with a look that reads at icon size and a personality in one line. Faces are what the store page, the icon, the packs and the result screens were missing last time.
7. **Voice.** Describe it and write sample lines: a tutorial line, a victory line, a defeat line, a store line, a notification. Short words; the lines will be translated into 50 languages.
8. **IP safety.** Name the famous franchises and genre conventions nearest to this theme, and for each, what the game does differently (names, ranks, symbols, costumes, layouts).
9. **Store listing drafts** to the stores' lengths. Every claim must be true of what the build will deliver from the template: check counts (units, levels, languages) against the status file, not memory. Never mention another game. Keywords without repeats.

## What went wrong last time

- No hero, no cast beyond one mentor portrait, a generic icon: "polished indie, not market tier".
- The chosen name was a plausible surname and place name in one launch language and no search had been done.
- The theme sat next to famous franchises; the rebuild had to rule out specific conventions after the fact.
- The listing overstated what free play could earn until an audit caught it.
- Portraits, a painted hero and a character-led icon need a human artist: that is an owner task, raised here, not discovered at the end.

## Limits

- Original names and characters only (doctrine 2). No real people, crests, religious or political symbols.
- The package and bundle identifiers are not decided here; the slug is a folder and package name only.
- A theme drawn from a living culture needs a native-speaker cultural review: owner task.

## A good record

The name, premise and cast could go on a store page tomorrow. The words for the home base, the player, alliances and rivals are fixed. Candidate names carry what the search found and a risk level. The listing claims nothing the template cannot do.
